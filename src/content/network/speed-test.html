Your connection at a glance

This is a rough performance estimate, not a detected ISP plan. Wi-Fi, proxies, server distance, and this short single-connection test can lower the result. Repeat over Ethernet with other traffic paused to compare your connection with your subscribed plan.

Understanding your results
  • Mbps means megabits per second. Divide by 8 to convert to MB/s. Download and upload tests transfer fixed amounts of data over a single connection and include request round-trip time.
  • HTTP latency is the average round-trip time of 5 requests after a warm-up request. It includes server processing time and is not ICMP ping. Jitter is the average absolute difference between consecutive latency samples.
  • Results reflect the connection from your browser to Cloudflare and depend on Wi-Fi, proxies, background traffic, and server location. Short samples may underestimate fast connections and do not represent your internet provider's maximum bandwidth.
  • The estimated download tier is the highest of 50, 100, 200, 300, 500, 1,000, or 2,000 Mbps for which measured download speed reaches at least 80% of the nominal rate. Below 40 Mbps, no tier is assigned; at 1,600 Mbps or above, the result is labeled 2 Gbps or higher. These are comparison bands, not proof of a broadband subscription.
  • For a rough comparison, HTTP latency below 50 ms is labeled low, 50–99 ms moderate, and 100 ms or more high. Jitter below 10 ms is labeled low, 10–29 ms moderate, and 30 ms or more high. These few idle HTTP samples do not predict latency under load, packet loss, or gaming performance.
  • If the connection fails, check your network, proxy, or browser blocking settings and try again. Results from completed stages are kept; unfinished measurements show “—”.
